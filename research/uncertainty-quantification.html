Uncertainty Quantification

This team develops Bayesian methods for choosing observations that reduce uncertainty in decision-relevant flood predictions.

Led by Xun Huan

Learning where to measure

The work connects uncertain rainfall and model parameters to uncertain inundation through uncertainty propagation and Bayesian inference. Optimal experimental design then asks where measurements would be most informative. Goal-oriented design targets quantities such as flood depth at a location of interest.

Detroit Arsenal studies investigate how monitoring objectives and timing influence recommended sensor locations. General methods in multifidelity estimation and sequential experimental design address the computational cost of these decisions. Current research also considers how monitoring recommendations can incorporate deployment constraints and field observations.

Research Focus

  • Propagate uncertainty from physical models to flood quantities of interest.
  • Compare parameter-oriented and prediction-oriented monitoring designs.
  • Develop efficient and adaptive methods for selecting measurements.

Connections Across the Project

The team uses flood simulations to evaluate information from potential observations. Collaboration with stakeholders and other teams helps define monitoring objectives and the practical conditions for field use.
