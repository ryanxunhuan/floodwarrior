Decision Science

This team studies how the presentation of flood risk shapes understanding, judgment, and decisions.

Led by Richard Gonzalez

How people make decisions under uncertainty

The team combines quantitative decision models with empirical studies of human behavior. Surveys in the Great Lakes region and central Texas examine risk perceptions and responses. Simulation games provide a controlled setting for studying how people use flood forecasts over repeated decisions.

A new flood-risk simulation distinguishes advisor and decision-maker roles and introduces changes in forecast uncertainty. The research examines how participants respond to more informative forecasts and how visual communication affects comprehension. These studies guide the design of uncertainty information for real decision contexts.

Research Focus

  • Study how people understand forecast variability and risk over time.
  • Compare observed decisions with quantitative decision models.
  • Evaluate communication through surveys, simulation games, and user studies.

Connections Across the Project

The team works with atmospheric and flood scientists on the information contained in forecasts, and with anthropology and visualization researchers on the context in which people interpret that information.
