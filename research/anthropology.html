Research

Anthropology

This team studies how institutional responsibilities and regional relationships shape flood decisions.

Led by John P. Ziker

Resilience in its institutional context

Ethnographic fieldwork, interviews, and site visits at Detroit Arsenal, in the surrounding region, and at Fort Hood identify how responsibilities, resources, institutional cultures, and coordination affect flood resilience. The research connects installation priorities with the wider social and environmental systems in which they sit.

The team uses a socio-ecological framework to examine uncertainty in multijurisdictional stormwater systems. It also develops ethnographic decision models grounded in how people describe their work. These insights help other teams shape studies and tools around stakeholder needs.

Research Focus

  • Understand decision roles and constraints within and across organizations.
  • Examine how military and civilian institutions coordinate around shared flood risks.
  • Support stakeholder engagement, research co-design, and public outreach.

Connections Across the Project

Anthropological research informs modeling objectives, visual tools, and behavioral studies. The StoryMaps communicate this research to a broader audience.
